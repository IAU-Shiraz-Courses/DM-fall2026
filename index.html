Data Mining

Fall 2026 · Islamic Azad University

This course looks at how raw, messy data becomes something a decision can be built on. We'll work through the full Knowledge Discovery in Databases pipeline — cleaning and preparing data, mining frequent patterns, building classifiers, and clustering unlabeled records — with a running thread of machine learning foundations and pattern recognition throughout. Expect practical, hands-on programming assignments in Python throughout the course.

Course description

Data Mining introduces the concepts, algorithms, and tools used to extract patterns and knowledge from large datasets. The course follows the standard Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD) process — from data cleaning and warehousing through pattern mining, classification, and clustering — and closes with a look at outlier detection and current research directions.

Grading combines a midterm, a final exam, Quera assignments, and a term project applying the full pipeline to a real dataset. Attendance is expected but not separately graded.

Syllabus & topics covered

Seven modules, taught in order, each building on the last.

What counts as a "pattern," the stages of the Knowledge Discovery in Databases process, and where data mining sits relative to statistics, databases, and machine learning.

  • KDD process overview
  • Data mining tasks: descriptive vs. predictive
  • Types of data: structured, semi-structured, streams

Real datasets are messy. This module covers making them usable before any modeling happens.

  • Missing values, noise, and outliers at the input stage
  • Normalization, discretization, and encoding
  • Feature selection and dimensionality reduction

How organizations store historical data for analysis, and how to query it along multiple dimensions.

  • Star and snowflake schemas
  • OLAP operations: roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice
  • ETL basics

Finding items that tend to occur together, and turning those co-occurrences into rules.

  • Apriori algorithm and candidate generation
  • FP-Growth and FP-trees
  • Support, confidence, and lift

Predicting a label for new records based on labeled examples.

  • Decision trees (ID3, C4.5, CART)
  • Naive Bayes classifiers
  • K-Nearest Neighbors and Support Vector Machines
  • Evaluation: precision, recall, ROC/AUC

Grouping unlabeled records by similarity, and deciding how many groups actually make sense.

  • K-Means and K-Medoids
  • Hierarchical clustering (agglomerative & divisive)
  • DBSCAN and density-based methods

Closing the course with anomaly detection and a survey of where the field is heading.

  • Statistical, distance-based, and density-based outlier detection
  • Mining data streams and text
  • A brief look at graph mining and recommender systems
